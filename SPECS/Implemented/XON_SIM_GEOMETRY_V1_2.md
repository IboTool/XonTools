## 1. Why this matters, and what E9 is actually testing

Everything tested so far has run on one geometry, the Sierpiński gasket. The model claims (master doc, Chapter 10) that its results belong to a **class**: self-similar graphs generated by inward refinement, with cycles. A result that holds only on the gasket could be a property of the triangle rather than of the class.

But "does everything pass on every geometry" is the wrong test, because the geometries differ in exactly the structural features the model says matter. Two features in particular:

- **Ramification.** A fractal is *finitely ramified* if its pieces can be separated by cutting finitely many points at every scale. The gasket and the Vicsek fractal are finitely ramified; the Sierpiński carpet is not (its pieces meet along whole edges). Finitely ramified fractals have exactly computable, massively degenerate spectra (spectral decimation); the carpet does not, and its walk dimension is known only numerically.
- **Large-scale cycles (closure).** The gasket has cycles at every scale. The carpet has cycles at every scale (around each hole). The Vicsek fractal, in the plus-shaped variant used here, is **tree-like above the smallest cell scale**: its pieces connect center-to-arm with no large loops. Chapter 10's requirement 3 (closure, so reflections return and interfere) is satisfied by the gasket and carpet, and fails for Vicsek at large scale.

So E9 is designed as a **prediction test**: each geometry gets a pre-registered expected outcome derived from its ramification and closure, and E9 passes if the outcomes match the predictions. This tests something stronger than generality: whether the features the model names as operative are the ones that actually matter.

## 2. Graph representation (applies to all new rules)

Use the **boundary-skeleton** representation, the same convention as the gasket: vertices at cell corners, edges along cell sides, shared vertices and edges merged (rounded-coordinate keys, tolerance 1e-9). No vertices are ever created strictly inside a removed cell. Every rule must extend `State` to new vertices by interpolation exactly as the gasket rule does.

## 3. New growth rules (`growth.py`)

### 3.1 `carpet` — Sierpiński carpet
- **Level 0:** one unit square (4 vertices, 4 edges).
- **Grow:** subdivide every leaf square into a 3×3 block of sub-squares and keep the 8 outer ones; the center sub-square is never created as a cell. Its boundary vertices still exist, since they are shared with the surrounding cells; what never exists is anything inside it.
- `labels["subregion"]`: at the first subdivision, tag each vertex by which of the 8 sub-squares it belongs to (vertices on shared boundaries: assign to the lowest-index sub-square, deterministically).
- **Structure:** infinitely ramified; cycles at every scale (around each hole); Hausdorff dimension ln 8 / ln 3 ≈ 1.893.

### 3.2 `vicsek` — Vicsek fractal (plus variant)
- **Level 0:** one unit square.
- **Grow:** subdivide every leaf square into a 3×3 block and keep the center and the four edge-midpoint sub-squares (5 of 9), discarding the four corners.
- `labels["subregion"]`: tag by which of the 5 sub-squares, as above.
- **Structure:** finitely ramified; 4-cycles only at the smallest cell scale, tree-like above it; Hausdorff dimension ln 5 / ln 3 ≈ 1.465. Serves as a **bridge case** between the gasket (self-similar with closure) and the tree control (no closure).

### 3.3 `sierpinski_p` — Sierpiński graphs S(p, n) (optional; replaces the n-gon rule)
The earlier draft's "generic n-gon subdivision" is withdrawn: connecting the midpoints of an n-gon for n ≥ 4 yields corner *triangles*, not corner n-gons, so the construction does not self-replicate. The correct, well-studied generalization of the gasket along the "number of corners" axis is the Sierpiński graph S(p, n) (Klavžar and Milutinović 1997):
- **Vertices:** words of length n over {1, …, p}.
- **Edges:** u ~ v iff there is an index h with u_t = v_t for t < h, u_h ≠ v_h, and for all t > h, u_t = v_h and v_t = u_h.
- Equivalently: S(p, n) is p copies of S(p, n−1), each pair joined by one edge between their extreme vertices; S(p, 1) = K_p.
- **Regression tests:** |V| = pⁿ; |E| = p(pⁿ − 1)/2.
- S(3, n) is closely related to the gasket graph (contracting the bridge edges recovers it), which gives an implementation cross-check. p = 4, 5 extend the family. Finitely ramified, with cycles at every scale for p ≥ 3; graph-metric dimension ln p / ln 2.
- Layout: place the p top-level copies on a regular p-gon, recursively.

## 4. Metrics (`metrics.py`)

Three independent dimension estimates, so the consistency check is not circular:

- `mass_dimension(g)`: d_f from mass–radius scaling in the **graph metric**. For 30 random centers at least `R_max` from the boundary, count vertices within graph distance r for r = 1…R_max; fit log M(r) vs log r.
- `walk_dimension(g)`: d_w from **mean-square displacement** of lazy random walks in graph distance: ⟨dist(X_t, X₀)²⟩ ∼ t^{2/d_w}. Fit over an intermediate window before finite-size saturation (default t from 10 to the time at which MSD reaches 25% of the squared diameter). This is a different observable from return probability; do not derive d_w from return probability.
- `spectral_dimension_est` (existing, from return probability).
- `einstein_residual = |d_s − 2 d_f / d_w|` (the standard relation d_s = 2 d_f / d_w).
- `degenerate_fraction(spec, m=3)`: fraction of eigenvalues lying in levels of multiplicity ≥ m (tol 1e-6).
- `plateau_persistence(spec_a, spec_b, m=3)`: Jaccard overlap of the sets of eigenvalues with multiplicity ≥ m at two consecutive levels (values matched within 1e-6).
- `seam_fraction(g, labels)`: edges crossing between different top-level sub-regions ÷ total edges. A direct measure of how narrow the seams between regions are.

Expected dimension values (reference table for E9c):

| geometry | d_f | d_w | d_s |
|---|---|---|---|
| gasket | ln 3/ln 2 ≈ 1.585 | ln 5/ln 2 ≈ 2.322 | 2 ln 3/ln 5 ≈ 1.365 |
| vicsek (plus) | ln 5/ln 3 ≈ 1.465 | ln 15/ln 3 ≈ 2.465 | 2 ln 5/ln 15 ≈ 1.189 |
| carpet | ln 8/ln 3 ≈ 1.893 | ≈ 2.10 (numerical; no closed form) | ≈ 1.80 |

## 5. Experiment E9 — Cross-geometry predictions

### 5.1 Sizes
- E9a, E9b, E9d: choose each geometry's level L so that 500 ≤ N ≤ 3,000 at level L; record L and N. E9a additionally uses levels L−2 and L−1, which may fall below the band.
- E9c: random-walk statistics need no eigendecomposition, so run at the largest level with N ≤ 50,000 to reduce finite-size error.

### 5.2 Pre-registered predictions and pass criteria

**E9a — Spectral self-similarity.** Statistics: `degenerate_fraction` at level L, and `plateau_persistence` between L−1 and L. Pearson r between normalized spectra is **reported only**, because it is not discriminating: a lattice's normalized spectrum is also nearly identical across sizes.

| geometry | prediction |
|---|---|
| gasket, vicsek, sierpinski_p | degenerate_fraction ≥ 0.5 and plateau_persistence ≥ 0.8 (finitely ramified ⇒ spectral decimation) |
| carpet | degenerate_fraction ≤ 0.3 (infinitely ramified; degeneracy from symmetry only) |
| lattice (control) | degenerate_fraction ≤ 0.3 |

Pass: every geometry matches its row.

**E9b — Structural individuation.** k-way spectral clustering (k = number of top-level sub-regions: 3 gasket, 5 vicsek, 8 carpet, p for sierpinski_p) on the lowest k eigenvectors including the constant; k-means with 20 restarts; purity = best matching to `labels["subregion"]`. Always use k-way clustering here; a single Fiedler vector cannot resolve more than two regions. Report `seam_fraction` alongside.

| geometry | prediction |
|---|---|
| gasket, vicsek, sierpinski_p, tree (control) | purity ≥ 0.85 (narrow seams) |
| carpet | exploratory: no confident prediction. Report purity and seam fraction |

Pass: every row with a stated prediction matches. Also report the across-geometry relation between seam_fraction and purity; the model predicts that narrower seams give cleaner individuation.

**E9c — Dimension consistency (correctness check on the simulation, not the model).**

| geometry | criterion |
|---|---|
| gasket, vicsek | each of d_f, d_w, d_s within 0.10 of the table in Section 4, and einstein_residual ≤ 0.10 |
| carpet | d_f within 0.10; d_w and d_s within 0.15 of the numerical values; einstein_residual ≤ 0.15 |
| sierpinski_p | d_f within 0.10 of ln p/ln 2; einstein_residual ≤ 0.15 |

If a geometry fails E9c, first rerun it at a larger level. If it still fails, treat it as a construction bug and resolve that before interpreting E9a, E9b, or E9d for that geometry.

**E9d — Coherence rise and closure (requires V1.1).**
- If `stuart_landau` is not registered: status `blocked_on_v1_1`.
- If E4b failed on the gasket: status `moot` (nothing to generalize).
- Otherwise run E4b's protocol on each geometry, with clusters from that geometry's k-way spectral clustering (k as in E9b), with the matched-N complete-graph control and the tree control. 5 seeds.

| geometry | prediction |
|---|---|
| gasket | as E4b (this row is E4b's replication) |
| carpet, sierpinski_p (p = 4) | significant rise (one-sided paired p < 0.05) **and** beats the complete-graph control |
| vicsek, tree | rise absent, or significantly smaller than the gasket's (one-sided p < 0.05 on the difference of end − start) |

Pass: every row matches. If vicsek and tree rise like the gasket, then closure (Chapter 10, requirement 3) is not what produces rich coherence, and the model's requirement list must be revised. Report that as a finding about the model, not a failure of the simulation.

## 9. What the results would mean

- **E9a and E9b matching predictions** would show that the spectral properties the model relies on (degenerate, self-similar spectra; individuation along narrow seams) track ramification, as spectral graph theory says they should. The model's Chapter 10 would then need a refinement: the favored class is *finitely ramified* self-similar graphs with closure, not self-similar graphs in general. That is a sharper and more defensible claim than the current one.
- **E9d matching predictions** (rise on gasket, carpet, and S(4, n); no or weaker rise on vicsek and tree) would be the first evidence that closure is the operative ingredient in the valence mechanism, which is exactly what the model's requirement list asserts.
- **E9d contradicting predictions** would be informative in a specific way: if vicsek rises like the gasket, closure is not what matters; if the carpet fails while the gasket passes, finite ramification is doing the work rather than closure. Either outcome revises a named requirement rather than leaving the model vaguely weakened.
- **E9c failing** points to the simulation, not the model, until shown otherwise.
